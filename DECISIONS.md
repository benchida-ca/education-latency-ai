# Decision log

*DRAFT for Ben to edit into his own words. Each entry gives the call, the alternatives, and why. The point of this file: a reviewer can see which judgments were mine.*

## Framing

1. **Measure adoption speed, not technology speed.**
   - Why: most AI debate is about capability; adoption gets less attention, and in education it usually means whether students use chatbots. I wanted to test the quiet assumption that schools adapt as fast as employers.
2. **Three timestamps: felt need, enactment, delivery.**
   - Why: this splits the clock into political latency and implementation latency. It maps onto the layers I worked in (political, administrative, operational), and it tests something I saw repeatedly in state government: passing a bill is not the same as students getting anything.
3. **"Clock speed" as the frame.**
   - Alternative: "the system is slow," which reads as cynical and isn't actionable.
   - Why: every channel has a clock, and the design question is routing each need to a fast enough channel.
4. **No "slowing down" hypothesis.**
   - What I dropped: I started with a hunch that education has slowed since the 1960s Master Plan era.
   - Why: two same-era cases can't test it, so I measure latency objectively and put the historical question in the research agenda.

## Cases

5. **Networking and cybersecurity.**
   - Rejected: web development, which never became a clearly credentialed pathway, so it wouldn't compare cleanly with networking. Also green jobs and health IT, which are sector overlays: more AI-like, but code-based data can't see them. Those moved to the research agenda and the "fast channel" counterpoint.
   - Rejected: 1960s data processing, because institution-level data requires ICPSR access.
6. **Cybersecurity felt need anchored around 2010.**
   - Why: that is when the sector became broadly in demand and when I saw community colleges and CSUs formalize programs. I report the earlier national-security wave (1998–2001) and the later workforce framing (about 2013) as a range.
7. **AI felt need anchored at ChatGPT (Nov 2022).**
   - Why: GPT-3 (2020) is when experts began converging; ChatGPT is when consensus formed broadly.

## Measurement

8. **The median learner, not the first adopter.**
   - Why: a pioneering college shows what is possible; the median shows what the system does.
9. **Weight by learners, not institutions.**
   - Why: I care about how most people learn, which is mostly community colleges and non-college pathways, not outlier institutions.
10. **Delivery means a credential with labor-market value.**
    - Why: a club or a single course is not delivery.
11. **Public and nonprofit delivery as the headline; for-profit reported separately.**
    - Why: for-profits exploited exactly this latency gap with fast credentials that often didn't pay off. A proxy was needed, and I checked it with earnings data rather than assuming it.
12. **Validate the value proxy with an existing federal standard.**
    - What: the gainful-employment earnings threshold, rather than inventing one.
13. **Felt need measured by layer, not one source.**
    - Why: elite press (NYT) is a proxy for elite thought, but it is too coarse for technical topics. I added more technical sensors: the Congressional Record and the Federal Register, by agency. Secretary of Labor speeches serve as qualitative anchors rather than counts, because their volume tracks administrations rather than need.
14. **Count community colleges that grant some bachelor's degrees as community colleges.**
    - Why: IPEDS files them as four-year schools, which would drop much of Florida's and Washington's systems.

## Validation

15. **Check federal codes against reality.**
    - Why: I suspected, from experience, that federal program codes miss how institutions actually adapt. Concentrations, generic state coding, and noncredit offerings are invisible.
    - What I did: a random sample of 50 universities, North Carolina's own approval records, and hand-checks.
16. **Report uncertainty and sensitivity.**
    - What: intervals on sample shares and a sensitivity table on every judgment call, so readers can see what the conclusions depend on.
